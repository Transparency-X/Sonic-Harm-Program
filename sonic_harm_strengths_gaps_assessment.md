# Sonic Harm Program — Strengths, Gaps & Coverage Assessment
**Assessment Date:** 2026-06-08
**Total Documents Assessed:** 32
**Framework Areas:** 15

---

## Executive Summary

This assessment evaluates the Sonic Harm program's output portfolio against a comprehensive 15-area framework covering the full lifecycle of a victim-centred, evidence-based, and scalable sonic harm response system.

- **Strong Coverage Areas:** 3 (Economic Impact Assessment, Offender Dynamics & Prevention, Global & Regional Scaling)
- **Partial Coverage Areas:** 6 (Legal & Policy Framework, Victim Support & Services, Community Protection & Resilience, Technology & Infrastructure, Governance & Accreditation, Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning (MEL))
- **Critical/Minimal Coverage Areas:** 4 (Training & Capacity Building, Cross-Border & All-Island Cooperation, Funding & Sustainability, Public Awareness & Communications)

**Overall Program Maturity:** The Sonic Harm program demonstrates exceptional strength in **Data & Evidence Management**, **Economic Impact Assessment**, and **Offender Dynamics Research**. However, there are critical gaps in **Training & Capacity Building** (zero coverage), **Community Protection & Resilience** (minimal), and **Public Awareness** (minimal) that must be addressed to achieve operational readiness.

## 1. Program Strengths

### 1.1 Research & Analytical Excellence
- **Comprehensive offender pathway mapping** (SH-OFF-001 through SH-OFF-006) with quantitative offender:victim ratios and intersectional community analysis (LGBTQI+, Traveller, allies).
- **Robust economic modelling** with Ireland-specific templates, national-scale worked examples, and global/EMEA extrapolation methodology (SH-ECO-001 through SH-ECO-007, SH-GLO-001 through SH-GLO-007).
- **Advanced data architecture** including federated protocols for 31 local authorities, chain-of-custody API design, and NGO accreditation frameworks (SH-INF-001, SH-ECO-005, SH-ECO-006).

### 1.2 Intersectional Design
- LGBTQ+ toolkit with integrated Torture harm type updates demonstrates commitment to marginalised community protections.
- Offender metrics v2.0 explicitly incorporates LGBTQI+, allies, and Traveller communities — rare in harm prevention research.
- All-island extension (Northern Ireland) shows political and jurisdictional foresight.

### 1.3 Scalability & Governance
- Clear version control and amendment tracking across all documents (v1.0 → v2.2 in global stream).
- Federated data protocol enables national aggregation without centralising sensitive victim data.
- NGO accreditation framework creates quality assurance and trust-building mechanisms.

### 1.4 Legal & Evidence Integrity
- Chain of Custody API addresses a critical gap in digital evidence handling for sonic harm cases.
- Reference to ICC *Prosecutor v. Al Mahdi* (2016) demonstrates international criminal law awareness.

## 2. Critical Gaps & Risks

### 2.1 CRITICAL: Training & Capacity Building (Area A10)
- **Coverage: 0% — ZERO documents mapped.**
- **Risk:** Without trained frontline workers (Gardaí, social workers, healthcare staff, housing officers), the entire registry, toolkit, and economic framework cannot be operationalised. Victims will report into a void.
- **Impact:** High. This is the single greatest barrier to program activation.

### 2.2 HIGH: Community Protection & Resilience (Area A6)
- **Coverage: Minimal.** Only LGBTQ+ toolkit and Noise Catalogue Strategy mapped.
- **Risk:** No community rapid response protocols, no 'Sonic Safe' venue accreditation, no bystander intervention training. Communities remain passive rather than empowered.
- **Missing:** Community resilience frameworks, neighbourhood watch adaptations, digital safe spaces.

### 2.3 HIGH: Public Awareness & Communications (Area A14)
- **Coverage: Minimal.** Only Noise Catalogue Strategy (which is technology-focused, not campaign-focused).
- **Risk:** If victims do not know the registry exists or how to report, data collection fails. Stigma reduction requires dedicated communications, not just toolkits.
- **Missing:** Media strategy, survivor storytelling platform, stigma reduction campaigns, ethical reporting guidelines.

### 2.4 MODERATE: Legal & Policy Framework (Area A1)
- **Coverage: Partial.** Registry definitions exist but no model legislation, prosecution guidelines, or sentencing frameworks.
- **Risk:** Without statutory footing, the registry and economic data may not be admissible or actionable in court.

### 2.5 MODERATE: Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning (Area A12)
- **Coverage: Partial.** Assessment approaches exist but no Theory of Change, no quarterly KPI dashboards, no academic research consortium.
- **Risk:** Program cannot demonstrate value to funders or improve iteratively without robust MEL.

### 2.6 MODERATE: Funding & Sustainability (Area A13)
- **Coverage: Minimal.** Only one document on funding programs detail.
- **Risk:** No diversified revenue strategy, no social impact bond feasibility, no reserve fund planning. Over-reliance on initial grants.

## 5. Strategic Recommendations

### 5.1 Immediate (Next 30 Days)
1. **Commission Training & Capacity Building stream** — This is the critical enabler for all other outputs. Without it, the program remains theoretical.
2. **Develop Community Protection framework** — Move beyond toolkits to active community resilience structures.
3. **Create Communications & Campaigns stream** — Separate from Noise Catalogue (which is product-focused).

### 5.2 Short-Term (3 Months)
4. **Integrate MEL into all existing streams** — Every document should have a Theory of Change and KPI framework attached.
5. **Strengthen Legal & Policy stream** — Move from registry definitions to actionable legislative drafting.
6. **Diversify Funding strategy** — Move beyond grant descriptions to revenue modelling and social enterprise feasibility.

### 5.3 Medium-Term (6–12 Months)
7. **Pilot all streams in one 'testbed' local authority** — Validate interoperability of registry, toolkit, economic template, and training before national rollout.
8. **Establish Victim & Community Advisory Panels** — Co-design all Phase 2 and 3 outputs to ensure lived experience drives iteration.
9. **Publish open-access research papers** — Build academic credibility and attract international partners for EMEA Observatory.
