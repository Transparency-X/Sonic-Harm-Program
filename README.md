# Sonic Harm 🌊

> **Sonic harm is expensive, unequally distributed, network-amplified, confidence-sensitive, and trauma-persistent. It cannot be solved by noise abatement alone. It requires a coordinated system of legal recognition, economic quantification, intersectional service design, offender prevention, and community resilience.**

> **A victim-centred, evidence-based, and scalable framework for understanding, measuring, and responding to sonic harm across Ireland, Europe, and beyond.**

## Overview

**Sonic Harm** is a multi-stream research and implementation program designed to address the under-documented and under-prosecuted phenomenon of intentional, weaponised, or negligent use of sound and acoustic environments to cause harm to individuals, families, communities, and businesses.

The program operates across **seven interconnected streams**, producing open-access documentation, data protocols, economic impact assessments, community toolkits, and technology frameworks. It is built for:

- **Victims and survivors** seeking recognition, support, and justice pathways
- **Frontline workers** (Gardaí, social workers, healthcare providers, housing officers) needing training and operational tools
- **NGOs and advocacy organisations** requiring accredited data partnership frameworks
- **Researchers and policymakers** demanding rigorous, scalable methodologies
- **Local and national authorities** seeking federated, GDPR-compliant evidence management

Sonic Harm is currently in **active alpha development** with a focus on the Irish context as the pilot jurisdiction, with explicit pathways for all-island (Northern Ireland) and EMEA scaling.

---

## The Problem

Sonic harm encompasses a spectrum of acoustic aggressions including:

- **Intentional acoustic harassment** (neighbourhood, domestic, institutional)
- **Weaponised sound deployment** (sonic weapons, crowd-control acoustics)
- **Environmental noise torture** (sleep deprivation, sensory overload)
- **Digital/remote acoustic attacks** (through devices, IoT, telecommunications)
- **Structural and institutional sonic neglect** (inadequate housing, care facilities, detention)

Despite its prevalence — particularly affecting marginalised communities (LGBTQ+, Traveller, disabled, elderly, migrants) — sonic harm lacks:

- Unified legal definitions and prosecution frameworks
- Standardised data collection and evidence handling
- Economic quantification for policy and funding arguments
- Accessible victim support pathways
- Frontline training and professional recognition

Sonic Harm exists to fill these gaps systematically.

- **Survivors and community members** — Lived experience advisory and co-design
- **Frontline professionals** — Training content, case studies, protocol refinement
- **Researchers** — Methodological critique, replication studies, peer review
- **Developers** — API implementation, PWA development, data visualisation

- **Policymakers** — Regulatory integration, funding advocacy, statutory adoption
